# Proof Notes for the \(k=3\) Case

## Lemma 1: Split-Polynomial Centrality

Let \(A\) be a reduced ring and let \(c\in A\). Suppose
\[
\prod_{\lambda\in \Lambda}(c-\lambda)=0
\]
where \(\Lambda\subseteq \mathbb F_p\) and the elements of \(\Lambda\) are distinct. Then \(c\) is central.

Proof. For each \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), Lagrange interpolation gives a polynomial \(e_\lambda(T)\in\mathbb F_p[T]\) such that \(e_\lambda(\mu)=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}\) for all \(\mu\in\Lambda\). Modulo \(\prod_{\mu\in\Lambda}(T-\mu)\), the polynomials \(e_\lambda\) are pairwise orthogonal idempotents and \(\sum_\lambda e_\lambda=1\), \(T=\sum_\lambda \lambda e_\lambda\). Evaluating at \(c\), the elements \(e_\lambda(c)\) are idempotents. Idempotents are central in a reduced ring, so \(c=\sum_\lambda \lambda e_\lambda(c)\) is central. \(\square\)

In particular, if \(p\equiv 1\pmod 3\) and \(c^3=1\), then \(c\) is central, because \(T^3-1\) splits into distinct linear factors over \(\mathbb F_p\).

## Lemma 2: Finite Norm Section

Let \(p\equiv 1\pmod 3\), let \(g\in\mathbb F_p[T]\) be irreducible of degree \(3\), and let \(i\in\{1,2\}\). Put \(q=p^3\). In the finite reduced algebra
\[
B=\mathbb F_p[S,T]/(S^{q-1}-1,\ g(T)),
\]
with automorphism \(\sigma_i(S)=S\), \(\sigma_i(T)=T^{p^i}\), there is a unit \(U\in B\) such that
\[
U\,\sigma_i(U)\,\sigma_i^2(U)=S.
\]

Proof. Decompose \(C=\mathbb F_p[S]/(S^{q-1}-1)\). Since the roots of \(S^{q-1}-1\) are exactly \(\mathbb F_q^\times\), every simple factor of \(C\) is either \(\mathbb F_p\) or \(\mathbb F_q\). Over a factor \(\mathbb F_p\), the algebra \(B\) is the cubic field \(\mathbb F_q\), and the norm \(\mathbb F_q^\times\to\mathbb F_p^\times\) is surjective. Over a factor \(\mathbb F_q\), the polynomial \(g\) splits and \(B\cong\mathbb F_q^3\), while \(\sigma_i\) cyclically permutes the three factors; the norm map \((x_0,x_1,x_2)\mapsto x_0x_1x_2\) is again surjective. Therefore \(S\) has a norm preimage on every simple factor. Combining these preimages by the Chinese remainder theorem gives \(U\in B\). \(\square\)

For fixed \(p\) and \(g\), the element \(U\) is represented by a concrete polynomial \(U_{g,i}(S,T)\). Verifying the displayed norm identity in \(B\) is a finite polynomial calculation, hence gives an equational certificate.

## Proposition 3: Monomial Wedderburn for \(p\equiv 1\pmod 3\)

Let \(p\equiv 1\pmod 3\) and \(q=p^3\). Then \(W_{p,3,T^p}\) and \(W_{p,3,T^{p^2}}\) have equational proofs.

Proof. It is enough to treat \(W_{p,3,T^{p^i}}\) for \(i=1,2\). Let \(A\) be a \(q\)-ring of characteristic \(p\), and suppose
\[
ba=a^{p^i}b.
\]
By Brandenburg's unit reduction for \(W_{p,k,f}\), it suffices to prove the claim on the central support where \(b\) is a unit; outside that support, \(b=0\). Decompose the element \(a\) by the central idempotents attached to the irreducible factors of \(T^q-T\). These idempotents are preserved by \(T\mapsto T^{p^i}\), because Frobenius permutes the roots inside each irreducible factor. Hence the relation \(ba=a^{p^i}b\) respects the same component decomposition. Linear factors give \(a^{p^i}=a\), so \(ba=ab\) there.

Now work on an irreducible cubic component \(g(a)=0\). The element \(a^{p^i}-a\) is a unit on this component because \(g\) and \(T^{p^i}-T\) are coprime. We show that a unit \(b\) satisfying \(ba=a^{p^i}b\) makes the component zero.

Let \(\sigma(a)=a^{p^i}\). Then \(\sigma^3(a)=a\), and
\[
b^3a=a^{p^{3i}}b^3=ab^3,
\]
so \(s=b^3\) commutes with \(a\); it also commutes with \(b\). By Lemma 2, choose \(u\) in the commutative subring generated by \(a\) and \(s\) such that
\[
u\,\sigma(u)\,\sigma^2(u)=s.
\]
Since \(b u=\sigma(u)b\), the element \(c=u^{-1}b\) satisfies
\[
c^3=u^{-1}\sigma(u)^{-1}\sigma^2(u)^{-1}b^3=1.
\]
Because \(p\equiv 1\pmod 3\), Lemma 1 makes \(c\) central. But
\[
ca=u^{-1}ba=u^{-1}a^{p^i}b=a^{p^i}c.
\]
Since \(c\) is central and a unit, \(a=a^{p^i}\). This contradicts the cubic component unless the identity element of the component is zero, because \(a^{p^i}-a\) is a unit. Hence the \(b\)-unit support on every cubic component is zero. Therefore \(ba=ab\) on all components.

All ingredients are finite polynomial identities for fixed \(p\): the idempotents for the factorization of \(T^q-T\), the Bezout inverse of \(T^{p^i}-T\) modulo each cubic \(g\), the norm-section identity of Lemma 2, and the idempotent decomposition of \(T^3-1\). Thus the proof is equational in Brandenburg's sense. \(\square\)

## Theorem 4: \(k=3\) Commutativity

For every prime \(p\), every characteristic-\(p\) ring satisfying \(x^{p^3}=x\) is commutative, and for each fixed \(p\) the proof can be written equationally.

Proof. If \(p=2\), \(p=3\), or \(p\equiv 2\pmod 3\), then \(\gcd(3,p^3-1)=1\), so Brandenburg's period criterion applies. If \(p\equiv 1\pmod 3\), Proposition 3 proves the two monomial constructive Wedderburn statements \(W_{p,3,T^p}\) and \(W_{p,3,T^{p^2}}\). Brandenburg's monomial sufficiency theorem then gives commutativity. \(\square\)
