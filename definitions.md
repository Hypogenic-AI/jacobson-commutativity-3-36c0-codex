# Definitions and Notation

Throughout, \(p\) is prime and \(q=p^3\). Rings are associative and unital, as in Brandenburg's paper; the usual idempotent-localization steps can be translated into equational proofs by replacing the identity with the relevant central idempotent.

## \(p^3\)-rings

A \(p^3\)-ring is a ring \(A\) satisfying
\[
px=0,\qquad x^{p^3}=x
\]
for every \(x\in A\). Such rings are reduced: if \(x^2=0\), then \(x=x^{p^3}=0\).

For positive exponents, \(x^{r+p^3-1}=x^r\). In particular, if \(x\) is a unit, then \(x^{p^3-1}=1\).

## Constructive Wedderburn Statements

For \(f\in \mathbb F_p[T]\), \(W_{p,3,f}\) denotes the following statement:

> In every \(p^3\)-ring \(A\), if \(a,b\in A\) satisfy \(ba=f(a)b\), then \(ba=ab\).

Brandenburg proves that to show all \(p^3\)-rings commutative, it is enough to prove
\[
W_{p,3,T^p}\quad\text{and}\quad W_{p,3,T^{p^2}}.
\]

## Frobenius Components

The polynomial \(T^{p^3}-T\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) is the product of all monic irreducible polynomials whose degrees divide \(3\). Since \(3\) is prime, these factors are linear or irreducible cubic.

On a component where \(g(a)=0\) for an irreducible cubic \(g\), the map
\[
\sigma_i(a)=a^{p^i},\qquad i=1,2,
\]
has order \(3\). On a linear component, \(a^{p^i}=a\).

## Cyclic Norm

If \(s\) is central and \(g(a)=0\) is an irreducible cubic component, define
\[
N_i(u)=u\,\sigma_i(u)\,\sigma_i^2(u)
\]
for \(u\) in the commutative subring generated by \(a\) and \(s\), with \(\sigma_i(s)=s\).

The key finite algebra fact used in the proof is:

> For fixed \(p\equiv 1\pmod 3\), fixed irreducible cubic \(g\), and \(i=1,2\), the norm map \(N_i\) is surjective on units after adjoining a central \(p^3\)-potent parameter \(s\). Equivalently, there is a polynomial \(U_{g,i}(S,T)\in\mathbb F_p[S,T]\) such that, in the finite quotient
> \[
> \mathbb F_p[S,T]/(S^{p^3-1}-1,\ g(T)),
> \]
> one has
> \[
> U_{g,i}(S,T)\,U_{g,i}(S,T^{p^i})\,U_{g,i}(S,T^{p^{2i}})=S.
> \]

For a fixed \(p\) and \(g\), this is a finite check and therefore can be converted into an equational certificate.
